When a key person leaves or a big project lands, finance leaders face the same question: bring in an interim, or recruit permanently straight away?

## When interim makes sense

- **Notice periods and leave.** Covering parental leave, long-term sickness or the gap while a permanent hire works their notice.
- **Projects.** System implementations, audits, restructures and acquisitions with a clear end date.
- **Peaks.** Month-end and year-end crunches where your team just needs extra hands.
- **Uncertainty.** If the shape of the role might change, an interim buys you time to decide.

## When to go straight to permanent

If the role is core to the team, well defined and long-term, a permanent hire usually costs less over a year and builds more stability. Good candidates also expect a clear, committed role.

## The combined approach

Many of our clients do both: an interim keeps the month-end running while we run a proper permanent search in the background. Nobody rushes a hire they will regret.

## What to have ready before you call

1. What has to get done in the next 30 days
2. Which systems and processes the person needs to know
3. Your budget for day rate or salary
4. Whether the role could be hybrid
